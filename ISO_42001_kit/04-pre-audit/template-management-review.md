## Section 1: Purpose

Management review is a mandatory requirement of ISO/IEC 42001:2023. Top management must review the organization's AI Management System (AIMS) at planned intervals to ensure its continuing suitability, adequacy, and effectiveness.

"Top management" means the person or group of people who direct and control the organization at the highest level — typically the CEO, Managing Director, or equivalent. Delegation to the AIMS Owner alone does not satisfy this requirement. The certification auditor will check attendance records.

The management review serves three functions that no other AIMS activity replicates:

1. **Strategic alignment.** It confirms the AIMS continues to reflect the organization's direction, risk appetite, and AI use patterns. AI tools and business priorities change. The management review is where those changes get formally acknowledged and acted on.

2. **Evidence of top management commitment.** ISO 42001 requires top management to demonstrate active involvement in the AIMS, not just sign a policy. Documented management review minutes are the primary evidence of that involvement.

3. **Continual improvement driver.** The outputs of the management review — decisions, resource allocations, and assigned actions — are the mechanism through which the AIMS improves over time. Without this review, the AIMS stagnates.

Completed minutes from this meeting are required documented information. Retain them in the AIMS document register and make them available to the certification auditor on request.

| 2.2 | Changes in external and internal issues relevant to the AIMS | Have any new AI systems been adopted since the last review? Have there been organizational changes (new departments, acquisitions, headcount changes) that affect the AIMS scope? | 2.3 | Trends in nonconformities and corrective actions | How many nonconformities were raised since the last review? Are any recurring? Have root causes been addressed, or are the same issues appearing repeatedly? | NCAR Log |

## Section 5: Required Outputs (Clause 9.3.3)

ISO 42001 specifies what the management review must produce. Before approving the minutes, the chairperson should confirm all required outputs are documented.

Use this checklist to verify completeness:

- [ ] **Continual improvement decisions** — At least one improvement opportunity discussed and a decision recorded (proceed, defer, or decline with rationale)
- [ ] **AIMS change decisions** — Any needed changes to the AIMS scope, policies, procedures, or controls have been identified and assigned
- [ ] **Resource decisions** — Resource requests reviewed and a decision recorded (approved, deferred, or declined with rationale and responsible owner)
- [ ] **Actions assigned** — All actions have a named owner and a specific due date (not "TBD")
- [ ] **Next review date confirmed** — A specific date is recorded, not just "in 12 months"
- [ ] **All required inputs addressed** — Each input in Section 2 was discussed and the discussion is documented in Section 4
- [ ] **Minutes approved** — Chairperson has reviewed and approved the minutes

If any item above is unchecked, the minutes are incomplete. An auditor reviewing these minutes will look for evidence that each required input was addressed. A general statement that "AIMS performance was reviewed" is not sufficient — the minutes must show what was reviewed and what was decided.

## Section 7: Review Frequency Guidance

### Minimum Requirement

ISO 42001 requires management review at "planned intervals." The standard does not specify a minimum frequency, but annual review is the accepted baseline for certification purposes.

### Recommended Schedule

| Organization Stage | Recommended Frequency | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| First two years of AIMS operation | Semi-annual (every 6 months) | The AIMS is new. Issues surface faster than an annual cycle can catch them. Semi-annual reviews build the management habit and generate more evidence of active oversight for the Stage 2 audit. |
| Established AIMS (year 3+) | Annual minimum | Once the AIMS is stable and the team is experienced, annual review is sufficient — provided trigger-based reviews occur when needed. |

### Trigger-Based Reviews

Certain events should prompt an unscheduled management review, or at minimum a documented management decision, outside the regular cycle:

- **Major AI system incident** — Any incident that caused material harm, regulatory scrutiny, or significant reputational impact
- **Significant regulatory change** — A new law or regulation takes effect that materially affects the organization's AI use (e.g., EU AI Act compliance deadline for a system in scope)
- **Adoption of a high-risk AI system** — Any new AI system that would be classified as high-risk under the EU AI Act or that processes sensitive personal data at scale
- **Significant organizational change** — Merger, acquisition, major restructuring, or entry into a new market that changes the AIMS context
- **Certification audit finding** — A major nonconformity raised during Stage 1 or Stage 2 audit that requires top management decision

For trigger-based reviews, the same agenda and minutes format applies. Note the trigger in the Document Metadata table under "Rationale for timing."
