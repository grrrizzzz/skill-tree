# Phase 4: Pre-Audit Readiness Review

> **Directory:** `04-pre-audit/` | **Internal Reference**

---

## What This Phase Accomplishes

The pre-audit phase is a structured internal review before the certification body arrives. Think of it as a dress rehearsal. You're stress-testing the AIMS documentation, checking that evidence is complete and accessible, and simulating the questions an auditor will ask. This phase also includes the client's first formal internal audit and management review, both of which are ISO 42001 requirements that must be completed before Stage 1 certification. Clients who skip or rush this phase tend to receive more nonconformities at Stage 2. A thorough pre-audit review is one of the highest-value things a consultant can do.

## Phase Inputs

Before starting Phase 4, you need:

- Complete AIMS documentation set from Phase 3 (all templates completed and reviewed)
- At least one cycle of AIMS operation (the system needs to have been running, not just documented)
- Client leadership available for the management review meeting
- Identified internal auditor (can be the consultant, a client staff member, or both)

---

## Phase Outputs

Phase 4 produces:

- **Internal Audit Report** (based on `template-internal-audit-checklist.md`) — documented evidence that the client conducted an internal audit, with findings and any corrective actions raised
- **Management Review Minutes** (`template-management-review.md`, completed) — documented evidence that top management reviewed the AIMS, with decisions and actions recorded
- **Pre-Audit Readiness Checklist** (`template-pre-audit-checklist.md`, completed) — a signed-off confirmation that the AIMS is ready for certification audit, or a list of remaining actions with owners and deadlines

---

## Estimated Duration

**2–3 weeks** for a typical SMB engagement. This includes scheduling and conducting the internal audit, facilitating the management review, reviewing all documentation against the readiness checklist, and addressing any findings before the certification body is engaged.

Don't compress this phase. If the internal audit surfaces significant gaps, the client needs time to address them before Stage 1. Build buffer into the timeline.

## Notes for This Phase

The internal audit is a formal ISO 42001 requirement (Clause 9.2), not just a preparation exercise. The audit must be planned, conducted by someone with appropriate competence, and documented. Findings must be reported to management. If the consultant conducts the internal audit, document the auditor's qualifications and independence from the areas being audited.

The management review (Clause 9.3) requires specific inputs: audit results, monitoring data, nonconformity status, stakeholder feedback, and AI system performance information. Help the client gather these inputs before the meeting, not during it. A management review that produces no decisions or actions is a red flag for auditors.

Use the pre-audit checklist as a go/no-go gate. If significant items remain open, delay engaging the certification body rather than proceeding with known gaps. Auditors will find them.
