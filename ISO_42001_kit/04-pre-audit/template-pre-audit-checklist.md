## Section 1: How to Use This Checklist

This checklist is your go/no-go tool before each stage of the ISO/IEC 42001:2023 certification audit. It is structured in two parts aligned with the two-stage certification process.

### The Two-Stage Certification Process

**Stage 1 — Document Review**
The certification body conducts a desk review of your AIMS documentation. They are checking that all required documents exist, are approved, are current, and are adequate in scope and content. Stage 1 is typically conducted remotely. The outcome is either a confirmation to proceed to Stage 2 or a list of findings that must be addressed first.

**Stage 2 — On-Site Audit**
The certification body visits your premises (or conducts a virtual equivalent) to verify that your AIMS is not just documented but actually implemented and effective. They will interview staff, request evidence of activities, and test whether your processes work as described. The outcome is a certification recommendation or a list of nonconformities.

### Completion Instructions

| Part | When to Complete | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Part A** | At least **4 weeks** before Stage 1 | Confirm document readiness |
| **Part B** | At least **2 weeks** before Stage 2 | Confirm implementation readiness |

### Status Definitions

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Yes** | Fully complete and evidenced |
| **Partial** | Exists but incomplete, outdated, or lacking evidence |
| **No** | Not yet started or does not exist |
| **N/A** | Not applicable to this organization's scope |

### Critical Items

Items marked **⚠️** are critical. The certification audit **will not proceed** if these items are incomplete. Any ⚠️ item marked Partial or No requires an immediate remediation plan with a named owner and a completion date before the audit is scheduled.

### Remediation Rule

Any item marked **No** or **Partial** requires a documented remediation plan before proceeding. Record the remediation action, owner, and target date in the Notes column. Track completion in your NCAR log.

### B.3 Common Stage 2 Audit Questions

*Use this section to prepare the client for the questions they are most likely to face during Stage 2. Run a mock interview session using these questions at least one week before the audit.*

**For the AIMS Owner:**

1. "Walk me through how you identified the AI systems in scope for your AIMS."
   - *Expected answer: Describes the AI system inventory process, discovery methods (IT review, department surveys, shadow AI identification), and how the inventory is maintained.*

2. "How did you determine which Annex A controls are applicable to your organization?"
   - *Expected answer: Describes the SoA development process — reviewing each of the 39 controls, assessing applicability based on the organization's role as an AI user, and documenting justifications.*

3. "Show me evidence that your AI risk assessment was conducted."
   - *Expected answer: Produces the risk assessment document AND supporting evidence (meeting notes, workshop records) showing the assessment was conducted through a structured process.*

4. "How do you ensure staff follow the Acceptable Use Policy?"
   - *Expected answer: Describes communication, training, acknowledgment records, and any enforcement mechanisms. Can provide examples.*

5. "What would you do if an AI system produced harmful or unexpected outputs?"
   - *Expected answer: Describes the incident response process — who to notify, how to document, how to assess impact, and how to raise an NCAR if required.*

6. "How do you manage AI vendor changes that might affect your risk profile?"
   - *Expected answer: Describes the process for reviewing vendor changes — monitoring vendor communications, reassessing risk when significant changes occur, updating the inventory and risk assessment.*

7. "Show me your most recent management review minutes."
   - *Expected answer: Produces the management review record showing required inputs were reviewed and decisions were documented.*

8. "How do you monitor the performance of your AIMS?"
   - *Expected answer: Describes the monitoring and measurement process — what metrics are tracked, how often, who reviews them, and how results feed into management review.*

**For Department Heads:**

9. "What AI tools does your team use, and what are the rules around using them?"
   - *Expected answer: Can name the AI tools in use, describe the Acceptable Use Policy requirements, and give examples of permitted and prohibited uses.*

10. "Has anyone in your team ever raised a concern about an AI system? What happened?"
    - *Expected answer: Describes the concern reporting mechanism and, ideally, can give an example of how it has been used.*

**For General Staff:**

11. "What is your organization's AI Policy, and what does it mean for your day-to-day work?"
    - *Expected answer: Can describe the policy in plain language and give examples of how it affects their work.*

12. "If you were unsure whether a particular use of an AI tool was allowed, what would you do?"
    - *Expected answer: Describes the escalation path — ask their manager, refer to the Acceptable Use Policy, or use the concern reporting mechanism.*

## Part C: Common Audit Failure Points

*This section documents the most frequent reasons ISO 42001 certification audits result in nonconformities or are delayed. Use this as a coaching guide in the final pre-audit briefing session with the client.*

---

### C.1 Stage 1 Failure Points (Document Review)

These issues cause Stage 1 findings that must be resolved before Stage 2 can proceed.

**C.1.1 — Statement of Applicability Incomplete or Inadequate**
The SoA is the document auditors scrutinize most carefully at Stage 1. Common failures:
- Controls listed without written justification (just "Yes" or "No" with no explanation)
- Justifications are generic and not specific to the organization's context
- Not all 39 controls addressed (some controls skipped or missed)
- Exclusions not justified (e.g., A.6.2.2 excluded without explaining that the organization does not develop AI)
- SoA not approved by management
- SoA uses incorrect Annex A numbering (A.5–A.13 instead of A.2–A.10)

*Remediation: Review every control entry. Ensure each has a specific, context-relevant justification. Have management formally approve the final version.*

**C.1.2 — Internal Audit Not Conducted or Auditor Not Independent**
ISO 42001 Clause 9.2 requires an internal audit before certification. Common failures:
- Internal audit not conducted at all (client assumed the consultant's gap analysis counted)
- Audit conducted by the AIMS Owner (not independent)
- Audit report does not reference ISO 42001 clauses as audit criteria
- Audit findings not documented
- No evidence that the audit actually occurred (no report, no notes)

*Remediation: Conduct a formal internal audit using an independent auditor (can be a colleague from another department or an external auditor). Issue a written report. Document all findings.*

**C.1.3 — Management Review Not Documented with Required Inputs**
ISO 42001 Clause 9.3 specifies what must be reviewed. Common failures:
- Management review conducted informally with no written record
- Meeting minutes exist but do not cover all required inputs (e.g., no reference to audit results, no performance data)
- No decisions or actions recorded
- Top management did not actually participate (delegated to AIMS Owner only)

*Remediation: Conduct a formal management review with a structured agenda covering all Clause 9.3 inputs. Record attendance, discussion points, decisions, and action items.*

**C.1.4 — Impact Assessments Missing for High Risk AI Systems**
ISO 42001 Clause 6.1.4 and Controls A.5.3/A.5.4 require impact assessments for AI systems that pose significant risk to individuals. Common failures:
- Impact assessments not conducted at all
- Impact assessments conducted only for some High Risk systems
- Impact assessment template completed but not signed off
- No evidence that the assessment was conducted (just a completed form with no supporting records)

*Remediation: Identify all AI systems classified as High Risk in the inventory. Conduct and document impact assessments for each. Obtain sign-off from the AI System Owner.*

**C.1.5 — Placeholder Text in Documents**
Documents submitted with unfilled placeholders are an immediate Stage 1 finding. Common failures:
- [Organization Name] not replaced throughout documents
- [YYYY-MM-DD] dates not filled in
- [TO BE COMPLETED] sections left blank
- Template instructions not removed

*Remediation: Conduct a final document review before submission. Search all documents for "[" to catch any remaining placeholders.*

**C.1.6 — Document Control Failures**
Common failures:
- Documents without version numbers or approval dates
- Different versions of the same document in circulation
- Documents not approved by the appropriate authority
- No document control register

*Remediation: Implement a simple document control register. Ensure every AIMS document has a version number, date, and named approver.*

---

### C.2 Stage 2 Failure Points (On-Site Audit)

These issues cause Stage 2 nonconformities that may delay or prevent certification.

**C.2.1 — Staff Unaware of AI Policy or Their Obligations**
This is the single most common Stage 2 failure. The certification body will interview staff at multiple levels. Common failures:
- Staff have never seen the AI Policy
- Staff know the policy exists but cannot describe what it requires
- Staff are unaware of the Acceptable Use Policy
- Training was delivered but staff cannot recall the content

*Remediation: Conduct a pre-audit awareness session. Brief all staff who may be interviewed. Ensure the AI Policy and AUP are accessible on the intranet. Run mock interview questions with key staff.*

**C.2.2 — Training Records Not Maintained**
Common failures:
- Training was delivered informally with no attendance records
- Records exist for some staff but not all relevant roles
- Training content is not documented (no agenda, no materials)
- Records are stored in personal files, not a central location

*Remediation: Establish a central training record. Ensure all AI governance training is documented with date, content, attendees, and delivery method.*

**C.2.3 — NCAR Process Not Operational**
Common failures:
- NCAR log exists but has never been used
- Internal audit findings were not converted to NCARs
- NCARs raised but root cause analysis not completed
- Corrective actions not tracked to closure

*Remediation: Ensure at least one NCAR has been raised and managed through the full process before Stage 2. Use internal audit findings as the source if no other nonconformities have occurred.*

**C.2.4 — Open Major Nonconformities from Stage 1**
Stage 2 cannot proceed to certification if Major NCs from Stage 1 are unresolved. Common failures:
- Stage 1 findings not fully addressed
- Corrective actions planned but not implemented
- Evidence of closure not documented

*Remediation: Address all Stage 1 findings before Stage 2. Document closure evidence for each finding. Confirm with the certification body that findings are accepted as closed.*

**C.2.5 — Risk Assessment Not Current**
Common failures:
- Risk assessment conducted during implementation but not reviewed since
- New AI systems added to the inventory but not assessed
- Significant organizational changes occurred without triggering a risk review

*Remediation: Review the risk assessment before Stage 2. Document the review date. Update the assessment for any new systems or significant changes.*

**C.2.6 — Supplier Assessments Not Conducted**
Common failures:
- Supplier assessment process documented but no assessments conducted
- Assessments conducted for some vendors but not priority vendors
- Assessment criteria not documented

*Remediation: Conduct assessments for all priority AI vendors before Stage 2. Document the assessment criteria and results.*

**C.2.7 — Monitoring Not Occurring**
Common failures:
- Monitoring procedure documented but no metrics collected
- Metrics collected but not reviewed or reported
- No evidence that monitoring results fed into management review

*Remediation: Collect at least one cycle of monitoring data before Stage 2. Ensure results are documented and referenced in the management review.*

**C.2.8 — Human Oversight Controls Not Implemented**
Common failures:
- Human oversight requirements documented in policy but not implemented in practice
- Staff do not know how to override or intervene in AI-assisted processes
- No evidence that human oversight checkpoints are being applied

*Remediation: Verify that human oversight controls are operational for all AI systems where they are required. Brief relevant staff on override procedures.*

### D.2 Readiness Thresholds

| Score | Recommendation |
