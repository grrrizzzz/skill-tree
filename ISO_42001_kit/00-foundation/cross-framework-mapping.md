# Cross-Framework Mapping Matrix — ISO/IEC 42001:2023

> **Current as of:** March 2026 | ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) | NIST AI RMF 1.0 | ISO/IEC 27001:2022

> **Document type:** Internal Reference — Consultant Use

---

## Purpose

This matrix is a reference tool for consultants helping clients understand how their ISO 42001 AI Management System (AIMS) work maps to other regulatory and framework obligations. It is designed to answer three common client questions:

1. **"We're subject to the EU AI Act — does our ISO 42001 work help us there?"** → Use Table 1.
2. **"Our US parent company uses NIST AI RMF — how does that align with what we're doing?"** → Use Table 2.
3. **"We're already ISO 27001 certified — what can we reuse?"** → Use Table 3.

**Important caveats — read before using:**

- Mappings in this document are **approximate and analytical**, not official cross-walks. No official equivalence or mutual recognition exists between ISO 42001 and the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, or ISO 27001 for certification purposes.
- The EU AI Act is a legal regulation with specific applicability rules based on AI system risk classification, role in the AI value chain, and sector. **This document does not constitute legal advice or a regulatory compliance assessment.** Clients with EU AI Act obligations should engage qualified legal counsel to determine specific applicability.
- NIST AI RMF is a voluntary framework. Alignment with ISO 42001 does not imply NIST AI RMF conformance or vice versa.
- ISO 27001 certification does not satisfy ISO 42001 requirements, and ISO 42001 certification does not satisfy ISO 27001 requirements. They are separate management systems with separate certification scopes.
- Mapping strength ratings (Direct / Partial / Indirect) reflect the consultant's analytical judgment, not any official determination.

## Table 1: ISO 42001 ↔ EU AI Act (Deployer Obligations)

> **Scope note:** This table focuses on **deployer obligations** under the EU AI Act — i.e., organizations that use AI systems in a professional context. Provider obligations (for organizations that place AI systems on the market) are out of scope for this toolkit, which serves AI-user organizations. High-risk AI systems (listed in Annex III of the EU AI Act) face the most extensive obligations. Consult qualified legal counsel for specific applicability determinations.

| ISO 42001 Clause/Control | ISO 42001 Requirement Summary | EU AI Act Article(s) | EU AI Act Requirement | Mapping Strength | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clause 4.1** — Context of the Organization | Identify internal and external factors that affect the organization's approach to AI governance, including regulatory environment and operational context | Art. 9 — Risk Management System; Art. 26(1) — Deployer Obligations | Deployers must implement risk management appropriate to their context; understand the AI system's intended purpose and operating environment | **Direct** | ISO 42001 Clause 4.1 context analysis directly supports the contextual understanding required before deploying high-risk AI. Document AI system purpose, operating environment, and regulatory context in the context analysis. |
| **Clause 4.2** — Interested Parties | Identify parties affected by or with interests in the organization's AI activities; document their requirements and expectations | Art. 26(1) — Deployer Obligations; Art. 9(2) — Risk Management Documentation | Deployers must understand the intended purpose and the population of users and affected persons; consider impacts on fundamental rights | **Partial** | ISO 42001 interested parties analysis covers stakeholders broadly. EU AI Act adds specific focus on affected persons and fundamental rights holders. Extend the interested parties register to explicitly identify individuals subject to AI-assisted decisions. |
| **Clause 5.2 / A.2.2** — AI Policy | Establish a formal AI policy committing to responsible AI, compliance, and ethical principles | Art. 9 — Risk Management System; Art. 26(1) — Deployer Obligations | Deployers must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures; establish governance for AI use | **Direct** | A documented AI policy is foundational to both frameworks. The ISO 42001 AI policy can explicitly reference EU AI Act compliance commitments. Ensure the policy addresses the deployer's specific obligations for any high-risk AI systems in scope. |
| **A.2.3** — Responsible AI Topics in AI Policy | Address fairness, transparency, accountability, human oversight, privacy, safety, and societal impact in the AI policy | Art. 9(2) — Risk Management; Art. 26(1) — Deployer Obligations | Risk management must address the specific characteristics and intended purpose of the AI system, including potential harms | **Partial** | Responsible AI principles in A.2.3 align with EU AI Act objectives but are broader. The EU AI Act is more prescriptive about specific risk categories for high-risk AI. Map responsible AI commitments to specific EU AI Act obligations during policy drafting. |
| **Clause 6.1.2** — Risk Assessment | Conduct systematic risk assessment for AI systems, identifying risks across technical, organizational, ethical, and societal dimensions | Art. 9 — Risk Management System for High-Risk AI | High-risk AI deployers must implement a risk management system covering identification, analysis, estimation, evaluation, and mitigation of risks throughout the lifecycle | **Direct** | The ISO 42001 risk assessment process directly satisfies the risk management system requirement for high-risk AI deployers. Ensure the risk assessment methodology explicitly addresses the risk categories specified in Art. 9 (health, safety, fundamental rights). |
| **Clause 6.1.4** — Impact Assessment | Evaluate potential impacts of AI systems on individuals, groups, and society; document mitigations and residual risks | Art. 27 — Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA) | Deployers of high-risk AI systems listed in Annex III (certain sectors) must conduct a FRIA before deployment; document and register results | **Partial** | ISO 42001 impact assessment and the EU AI Act FRIA address overlapping concerns but differ in scope and formality. The FRIA has specific legal requirements (registration, content) that go beyond ISO 42001's impact assessment. Use the ISO 42001 impact assessment as the foundation and extend it to meet FRIA requirements where applicable. |
| **A.5.2** — AI System Risk Assessment | Identify and assess risks from AI systems across technical, organizational, ethical, and societal dimensions; conduct initial and periodic reassessments | Art. 9 — Risk Management System; Art. 26(5) — Post-Market Monitoring | Risk management must be ongoing; deployers must monitor AI system performance and report serious incidents | **Direct** | A.5.2 provides the operational control that implements Clause 6.1.2 risk assessment. Periodic reassessment under A.5.2 aligns with the EU AI Act's requirement for ongoing risk management throughout the AI system lifecycle. |
| **A.5.3** — AI System Impact Assessment | Evaluate impacts on individuals and groups, including potential harms, discrimination, and privacy effects; document mitigations | Art. 27 — FRIA; Art. 9(2) — Risk Management Documentation | FRIA must assess impacts on fundamental rights; document methodology, results, and mitigations | **Partial** | A.5.3 impact assessment covers similar ground to the FRIA but is not a legal substitute. For clients subject to Art. 27, use A.5.3 as the analytical foundation and produce a separate FRIA document meeting the specific legal requirements. |
| **Clause 6.1.6** — Statement of Applicability (SoA) | Document which Annex A controls are applicable, with justification for inclusion or exclusion | Art. 9(2) — Risk Management Documentation | Risk management documentation must describe the risk management system, measures taken, and residual risks | **Partial** | The SoA demonstrates systematic control selection, which supports the documentation requirements of Art. 9(2). Not a direct substitute — the SoA documents control applicability while Art. 9(2) requires documentation of risk management measures and outcomes. |
| **Clause 7.2 / A.4.3** — Competence | Ensure personnel involved in AI activities have required competencies; identify gaps and provide training | Art. 26(6) — Human Oversight Competence; Art. 4 — AI Literacy | Deployers must assign human oversight to persons with necessary competence, training, and authority; providers and deployers must ensure AI literacy of their staff | **Direct** | ISO 42001 competence requirements directly satisfy the EU AI Act's AI literacy (Art. 4) and human oversight competence (Art. 26(6)) requirements. Competency records maintained under ISO 42001 serve as evidence of compliance with Art. 4. |
| **Clause 7.3 / A.4.4** — Awareness | Ensure personnel are aware of the AI policy, relevant risks, ethical considerations, and their responsibilities | Art. 4 — AI Literacy; Art. 26(6) — Human Oversight | Staff must have sufficient AI literacy to understand AI system capabilities, limitations, and their oversight responsibilities | **Direct** | Awareness programs under ISO 42001 directly address the AI literacy requirement. Ensure awareness content covers the specific AI systems in use, their limitations, and the human oversight procedures required under Art. 26. |
| **Clause 8.1** — Operational Planning and Control | Plan, implement, and control processes needed to meet AI governance requirements; manage operational changes | Art. 26(1)–(5) — Deployer Operational Obligations | Deployers must: use AI systems per instructions; implement human oversight; ensure input data relevance; monitor performance; inform affected persons; suspend use if risks identified | **Direct** | ISO 42001 operational planning provides the management system structure for implementing the specific deployer obligations in Art. 26. Map each Art. 26 obligation to a specific operational procedure or control during implementation. |
| **A.8.2** — Informing Interested Parties About AI System Interaction | Notify individuals when they are interacting with an AI system through appropriate disclosure mechanisms | Art. 13 — Transparency for High-Risk AI; Art. 50 — Transparency Obligations for Certain AI Systems | High-risk AI deployers must ensure transparency to affected persons; certain AI systems (chatbots, deepfakes) have specific disclosure requirements | **Direct** | A.8.2 directly implements the transparency disclosure requirements. For high-risk AI, ensure disclosures meet the specific content requirements of Art. 13. For chatbots and emotion recognition systems, Art. 50 imposes additional disclosure obligations. |
| **A.8.3** — Informing Interested Parties About AI Outcomes | Enable understanding of AI-driven decisions; communicate explanations of outcomes and key decision factors | Art. 13 — Transparency; Art. 86 — Right to Explanation | High-risk AI systems must provide sufficient transparency to enable users to interpret outputs; affected persons have a right to explanation for certain AI-assisted decisions | **Partial** | A.8.3 aligns with transparency requirements but the EU AI Act's right to explanation (Art. 86) has specific legal content requirements. Ensure AI outcome communications meet the explainability standards required for the specific AI system type. |
| **A.9.5** — Human Oversight Aspects | Ensure appropriate human control through override capability, intervention points, and defined oversight levels | Art. 14 — Human Oversight for High-Risk AI | High-risk AI systems must be designed and deployed to allow effective human oversight; deployers must implement oversight measures specified in the instructions for use | **Direct** | A.9.5 is the primary ISO 42001 control for implementing Art. 14 human oversight requirements. Document the specific oversight level (human-in-the-loop, human-on-the-loop, human-in-command) for each high-risk AI system and ensure it meets the Art. 14 standard. |
| **A.10.2** — Suppliers of AI System Components | Manage AI component suppliers through selection criteria, contractual requirements, ongoing assessment, and performance monitoring | Art. 25 — Obligations of Importers and Distributors; Art. 26(7) — Deployer-Provider Cooperation | Deployers must cooperate with providers to ensure compliance; providers must supply necessary information; supply chain responsibilities are allocated by contract | **Direct** | A.10.2 supplier management directly supports the supply chain obligations in Art. 25 and Art. 26(7). Ensure supplier contracts include EU AI Act compliance representations, information-sharing obligations, and incident notification requirements. |
| **Clause 9.1** — Monitoring, Measurement, Analysis, and Evaluation | Monitor and measure AI system performance and AIMS effectiveness; analyze and evaluate results | Art. 26(5) — Post-Market Monitoring | Deployers must monitor AI system operation and report serious incidents and malfunctions to the provider; maintain logs where technically feasible | **Direct** | ISO 42001 monitoring requirements directly implement the post-market monitoring obligations for deployers. Ensure monitoring procedures include the specific performance indicators and incident detection criteria required under Art. 26(5). |
| **Clause 10.1** — Nonconformity and Corrective Action | Identify nonconformities, take corrective action, and prevent recurrence; maintain records | Art. 73 — Reporting of Serious Incidents | Deployers must report serious incidents or malfunctions to the provider and, where required, to national market surveillance authorities | **Partial** | ISO 42001 corrective action covers internal nonconformities broadly. Art. 73 adds a specific external reporting obligation for serious incidents. Extend the corrective action procedure to include an incident classification step that triggers Art. 73 reporting where applicable. |

> **EU AI Act applicability reminder:** The obligations in this table apply based on AI system risk classification. High-risk AI systems (Annex III of the EU AI Act) face the most extensive obligations. General-purpose AI systems, prohibited AI systems, and limited-risk AI systems have different obligation profiles. **Consult qualified legal counsel for specific applicability determinations.** This mapping does not constitute a legal compliance assessment.

## Table 2: ISO 42001 ↔ NIST AI RMF 1.0

> **Scope note:** NIST AI RMF 1.0 is a voluntary framework organized around four functions: **GOVERN**, **MAP**, **MEASURE**, and **MANAGE**. Each function contains categories (e.g., GOVERN 1.1) and subcategories. This table maps ISO 42001 clauses and controls to the most relevant NIST AI RMF categories. Mapping strength reflects the degree of conceptual and operational overlap.

| ISO 42001 Clause/Control | NIST AI RMF Function | NIST AI RMF Category | Mapping Strength | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clause 4.1** — Context of the Organization | MAP | MAP 1.1 — Context is established for the AI risk assessment | **Direct** | ISO 42001 context analysis directly corresponds to MAP 1.1's requirement to establish organizational context before conducting AI risk assessments. Use the context analysis outputs as inputs to the MAP function. |
| **Clause 4.2** — Interested Parties | MAP | MAP 1.6 — Risks or impacts from third-party entities are understood | **Partial** | Interested parties analysis covers stakeholders broadly; MAP 1.6 focuses specifically on third-party risk. Supplement with A.10.2 supplier management for full MAP 1.6 coverage. |
| **Clause 5.1** — Leadership and Commitment | GOVERN | GOVERN 1.1 — Policies, processes, procedures, and practices across the organization related to the mapping, measuring, and managing of AI risks are in place | **Direct** | Top management commitment under ISO 42001 Clause 5.1 directly supports GOVERN 1.1's requirement for organizational-level AI risk governance. Leadership accountability is a shared requirement. |
| **Clause 5.2 / A.2.2** — AI Policy | GOVERN | GOVERN 1.1 — Policies and processes for AI risk; GOVERN 1.2 — Accountability structures are in place | **Direct** | The ISO 42001 AI policy is the primary instrument for implementing GOVERN 1.1 and 1.2. Ensure the AI policy explicitly addresses accountability structures and risk governance processes to satisfy both frameworks simultaneously. |
| **A.2.3** — Responsible AI Topics in AI Policy | GOVERN | GOVERN 1.3 — Organizational teams are committed to a culture that considers and communicates AI risk | **Partial** | Responsible AI principles in A.2.3 (fairness, transparency, accountability) align with GOVERN 1.3's culture requirements. NIST AI RMF places additional emphasis on organizational culture and psychological safety for raising AI concerns. |
| **Clause 5.3 / A.3.2** — Roles and Responsibilities | GOVERN | GOVERN 2.1 — Roles and responsibilities and organizational accountability for implementing and managing AI risks are documented; GOVERN 2.2 — The organization's personnel and partners receive AI risk management training | **Direct** | ISO 42001 role definition requirements directly satisfy GOVERN 2.1 accountability documentation. Ensure role descriptions explicitly reference AI risk management responsibilities to create clear GOVERN 2.1 evidence. |
| **Clause 6.1.2** — Risk Assessment | MAP | MAP 1.1 — Context established; MAP 1.5 — Organizational risk tolerances are determined and communicated | **Direct** | ISO 42001 risk assessment methodology directly implements the MAP function's risk identification and analysis requirements. Document risk tolerance thresholds in the risk assessment methodology to satisfy MAP 1.5. |
| **Clause 6.1.4** — Impact Assessment | MAP | MAP 5.1 — Likelihood and magnitude of each identified impact based on expected use; MAP 5.2 — Practices and personnel for supporting affected communities are in place | **Direct** | ISO 42001 impact assessment directly corresponds to MAP 5.1 and 5.2. The impact assessment should explicitly quantify or qualify likelihood and magnitude to satisfy MAP 5.1's analytical requirements. |
| **A.5.2** — AI System Risk Assessment | MAP | MAP 2.1 — Scientific basis for AI risk identification; MAP 2.2 — Scientific basis for risk categories | **Partial** | A.5.2 provides the operational risk assessment process. NIST AI RMF MAP 2.1/2.2 additionally emphasizes the scientific and empirical basis for risk identification. Supplement A.5.2 with references to relevant research, benchmarks, or empirical evidence for identified risks. |
| **A.5.3** — AI System Impact Assessment | MAP | MAP 5.1 — Likelihood and magnitude of impacts; MAP 5.2 — Practices for supporting affected communities | **Direct** | A.5.3 is the primary Annex A control for MAP 5.x. Ensure impact assessments document both the likelihood and magnitude dimensions required by MAP 5.1, not just a binary harm/no-harm determination. |
| **A.5.4** — Impact of AI System Documentation | GOVERN | GOVERN 1.7 — Processes for AI risk management are established and documented | **Partial** | A.5.4 documentation requirements support GOVERN 1.7's process documentation requirements. Ensure impact assessment records are integrated into the broader AI risk management documentation system. |
| **Clause 7.2 / A.4.3** — Competence | GOVERN | GOVERN 4.1 — Organizational teams are committed to a culture that considers and communicates AI risk; GOVERN 4.2 — Organizational teams document the risks of AI and are committed to transparent reporting | **Direct** | Competency requirements under ISO 42001 directly support GOVERN 4.1 and 4.2. Competency records demonstrate that personnel have the skills to identify, assess, and communicate AI risks as required by GOVERN 4.x. |
| **Clause 7.3 / A.4.4** — Awareness | GOVERN | GOVERN 4.1 — Culture that considers and communicates AI risk; GOVERN 6.2 — Policies and procedures are in place for AI risk management across the supply chain | **Partial** | Awareness programs address GOVERN 4.1 culture requirements. Extend awareness content to include supply chain AI risks to also address GOVERN 6.2. |
| **A.4.5** — Consultation | GOVERN | GOVERN 4.2 — Organizational teams document risks and are committed to transparent reporting; GOVERN 5.1 — Organizational policies and practices are in place for AI risk management | **Partial** | Consultation requirements in A.4.5 support the stakeholder engagement aspects of GOVERN 4.2 and 5.1. Document consultation activities as evidence of stakeholder-informed risk governance. |
| **Clause 8.1** — Operational Planning and Control | MANAGE | MANAGE 1.1 — A determination is made as to whether the AI system achieves its intended purpose; MANAGE 2.1 — Resources required to manage AI risks are taken | **Direct** | Operational planning under ISO 42001 directly implements the MANAGE function's requirement to operationalize risk treatment decisions. Map operational procedures to specific MANAGE categories during implementation. |
| **A.6.2.5** — Deployment of AI System | MANAGE | MANAGE 1.3 — Responses to the AI risks deemed high priority are developed; MANAGE 2.1 — Resources required to manage AI risks are taken | **Partial** | Deployment planning under A.6.2.5 supports MANAGE 1.3 and 2.1 by ensuring high-priority risks are addressed before deployment. Include risk treatment verification as a deployment gate criterion. |
| **A.6.2.6** — Operation and Monitoring of AI System | MEASURE | MEASURE 2.5 — AI system performance is measured; MEASURE 2.6 — AI system performance is evaluated for bias and fairness | **Direct** | A.6.2.6 operational monitoring directly implements MEASURE 2.5 and 2.6. Ensure monitoring metrics include both performance and bias/fairness indicators to satisfy both ISO 42001 and NIST AI RMF requirements simultaneously. |
| **A.9.5** — Human Oversight Aspects | MANAGE | MANAGE 2.2 — Mechanisms to sustain the value of deployed AI systems are evaluated; MANAGE 2.4 — Residual risks are documented | **Direct** | Human oversight under A.9.5 is a primary risk treatment mechanism that directly implements MANAGE 2.2 and 2.4. Document the specific oversight level and intervention criteria as evidence of residual risk management. |
| **Clause 9.1** — Monitoring, Measurement, Analysis, and Evaluation | MEASURE | MEASURE 2.5 — AI system performance is measured; MEASURE 2.6 — Bias and fairness evaluation; MEASURE 2.7 — AI system security and resilience | **Direct** | ISO 42001 monitoring requirements directly implement the MEASURE function. Ensure the monitoring program covers all MEASURE 2.x dimensions: performance, bias, security, and resilience. |
| **Clause 9.2** — Internal Audit | MEASURE | MEASURE 4.1 — Measurement approaches for identifying AI risks are examined for completeness and accuracy | **Partial** | Internal audit under ISO 42001 supports MEASURE 4.1 by providing independent verification of measurement approaches. Extend audit scope to explicitly assess the completeness and accuracy of AI risk measurement methods. |
| **Clause 9.3** — Management Review | GOVERN | GOVERN 1.4 — Organizational teams are committed to a culture that considers and communicates AI risk; GOVERN 5.2 — Organizational risk tolerance is determined and communicated | **Partial** | Management review provides the governance forum for GOVERN 1.4 culture and GOVERN 5.2 risk tolerance decisions. Ensure management review agenda includes explicit discussion of AI risk tolerance and organizational culture indicators. |
| **Clause 10.1** — Nonconformity and Corrective Action | MANAGE | MANAGE 3.1 — Responses to identified risks are developed; MANAGE 3.2 — Incidents and errors are responded to | **Direct** | Corrective action under ISO 42001 directly implements MANAGE 3.1 and 3.2. Ensure the corrective action procedure includes AI-specific incident classification and response criteria aligned with MANAGE 3.2. |
| **Clause 10.2** — Continual Improvement | MANAGE | MANAGE 4.1 — Post-deployment AI risks are monitored; MANAGE 4.2 — Measurable improvements are demonstrated | **Direct** | Continual improvement under ISO 42001 directly corresponds to MANAGE 4.1 and 4.2. The improvement cycle (PDCA) provides the operational mechanism for demonstrating measurable AI risk management improvement over time. |
| **A.10.2** — Suppliers of AI System Components | GOVERN | GOVERN 6.1 — Policies and procedures are in place for AI risk management across the supply chain; GOVERN 6.2 — Policies and procedures are in place for AI risk management across the supply chain | **Direct** | A.10.2 is the primary Annex A control for GOVERN 6.x supply chain requirements. Supplier assessment criteria, contractual requirements, and ongoing monitoring under A.10.2 directly implement GOVERN 6.1 and 6.2. |
| **A.10.3** — Shared ML Models | GOVERN | GOVERN 6.1 — Supply chain AI risk policies; MAP 2.2 — Scientific basis for risk categories | **Partial** | Shared model management under A.10.3 addresses a specific supply chain risk category. Supplement with MAP 2.2 analysis of the specific risk characteristics of shared/pre-trained models (bias inheritance, provenance uncertainty). |

## Table 3: ISO 42001 ↔ ISO 27001 Leverage Opportunities

> **Purpose:** This table is specifically for clients who already hold ISO 27001 certification. It identifies where existing ISO 27001 processes, documentation, and controls can be directly reused or extended for ISO 42001 — reducing implementation effort and avoiding duplication. Estimated overall effort reduction for ISO 27001-certified clients: **30–40%** compared to a greenfield ISO 42001 implementation.

> **Important:** ISO 27001 certification does not satisfy ISO 42001 requirements. These are separate management systems with separate certification scopes. This table identifies leverage opportunities, not equivalences.

| ISO 42001 Clause/Control | ISO 42001 Requirement | ISO 27001 Clause/Control | Leverage Opportunity | Action Required |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clause 4.1** — Context of the Organization | Identify internal and external factors affecting AI governance; understand the organizational context for the AIMS | ISO 27001 Clause 4.1 — Context of the Organization | **Direct reuse** — The context analysis already exists. ISO 27001 clients have documented their internal and external context, including regulatory environment, organizational structure, and strategic objectives. | Extend the existing context analysis document to explicitly include AI-specific factors: AI systems in use, AI regulatory environment (EU AI Act applicability), AI-specific internal factors (AI strategy, AI capabilities). Add an "AI Context" section rather than creating a new document. |
| **Clause 4.2** — Interested Parties | Identify parties affected by or with interests in AI activities; document their requirements | ISO 27001 Clause 4.2 — Understanding the Needs and Expectations of Interested Parties | **Direct reuse** — The interested parties register already exists. ISO 27001 clients have identified regulators, customers, employees, and suppliers as interested parties. | Add AI-specific interested parties to the existing register: individuals subject to AI-assisted decisions, AI system providers, AI regulators (where applicable). Document AI-specific requirements for each party (e.g., transparency expectations, data rights). |
| **Clause 4.3** — Scope | Define the boundaries and applicability of the AIMS | ISO 27001 Clause 4.3 — Determining the Scope of the ISMS | **Partial reuse** — The scoping methodology and documentation format are reusable. The ISMS scope document provides a template for the AIMS scope document. | Create a separate AIMS scope document using the ISMS scope as a template. The AIMS scope will typically be narrower (specific AI systems) or differently bounded than the ISMS scope. Do not simply extend the ISMS scope to cover AI — maintain separate scope documents for audit clarity. |
| **Clause 5.2 / A.2.2** — AI Policy | Establish a formal AI policy committing to responsible AI, compliance, and ethical principles | ISO 27001 Clause 5.2 — Information Security Policy | **Partial reuse** — The policy structure, approval process, and communication mechanism are reusable. The IS Policy demonstrates that the organization can produce and maintain a management-level policy. | Create a separate AI Policy document (do not embed AI governance in the IS Policy — auditors prefer distinct policies for distinct management systems). Use the IS Policy as a structural template. The AI Policy must address AI-specific topics (responsible AI principles, AI objectives, AI risk appetite) that are not covered by the IS Policy. |
| **Clause 6.1.2** — Risk Assessment | Conduct systematic risk assessment for AI systems | ISO 27001 Clause 6.1.2 — Information Security Risk Assessment | **Partial reuse** — The risk assessment methodology, risk register format, and risk scoring criteria are reusable. ISO 27001 clients have an established, audited risk assessment process. | Extend the existing risk methodology to include AI-specific risk categories: algorithmic bias, model drift, AI supply chain risk, over-reliance, and AI-specific data risks. Create an AI Risk Register as a separate register (or clearly delineated section) within the existing risk management framework. Ensure AI risk criteria are calibrated to AI-specific harm types. |
| **Clause 6.1.3** — Risk Treatment | Select and implement controls to address identified AI risks | ISO 27001 Clause 6.1.3 — Information Security Risk Treatment | **Partial reuse** — The risk treatment process, treatment option framework (accept/avoid/transfer/mitigate), and treatment plan format are reusable. | Create an AI Risk Treatment Plan using the existing RTP format. Map AI risk treatment decisions to Annex A controls (SoA). Note that AI risk treatment options include AI-specific measures (human oversight levels, usage restrictions, vendor contractual controls) not present in ISO 27001 treatment options. |
| **Clause 6.1.6** — Statement of Applicability (SoA) | Document which Annex A controls are applicable with justification | ISO 27001 Clause 6.1.3(d) — Statement of Applicability | **Partial reuse** — The SoA concept, format, and documentation discipline are directly transferable. ISO 27001 clients understand what an SoA is and how to maintain it. | Create a separate ISO 42001 SoA document. Do not combine with the ISO 27001 SoA — they reference different Annex A controls from different standards. Use the ISO 27001 SoA as a format template. The ISO 42001 SoA references the 39 Annex A controls from ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (A.2–A.10). |
| **Clause 7.1** — Resources | Identify and provide resources needed for the AIMS | ISO 27001 Clause 7.1 — Resources | **Direct reuse** — Resource planning processes and documentation formats are directly reusable. | Extend existing resource planning to include AI governance resources: AI risk assessment tools, AI monitoring capabilities, AI-specific training budget, and personnel time for AIMS activities. |
| **Clause 7.4** — Communication | Determine internal and external communication needs for the AIMS | ISO 27001 Clause 7.4 — Communication | **Direct reuse** — The communication planning process and stakeholder communication mechanisms are reusable. | Add AI-specific communication requirements to the existing communication plan: AI policy communication, AI incident notifications, AI transparency disclosures to affected persons. |
| **Clause 7.5** — Documented Information | Control and maintain required AIMS documented information | ISO 27001 Clause 7.5 — Documented Information | **Direct reuse** — The document control procedure, version control system, and records management process are fully reusable. | Extend the existing document control procedure to cover AIMS documents. Add AIMS document types to the document register. No new document control infrastructure is needed — simply register AIMS documents within the existing system. This is one of the highest-leverage reuse opportunities. |
| **Clause 8.1** — Operational Planning and Control | Plan, implement, and control processes for AI governance | ISO 27001 Clause 8.1 — Operational Planning and Control | **Partial reuse** — The operational planning discipline and change management processes are reusable. | Develop AI-specific operational procedures (AI acceptable use, AI incident response, AI vendor management) using existing operational procedure templates. Integrate AI operational controls into existing change management processes. |
| **Clause 9.1** — Monitoring, Measurement, Analysis, and Evaluation | Monitor and measure AIMS performance and AI system performance | ISO 27001 Clause 9.1 — Monitoring, Measurement, Analysis, and Evaluation | **Partial reuse** — The monitoring framework, KPI methodology, and performance reporting processes are reusable. | Add AI-specific performance indicators to the existing monitoring framework: AI system availability, AI output quality metrics, AI incident rates, AI policy compliance rates. Extend existing dashboards or reporting mechanisms rather than building new ones. |
| **Clause 9.2** — Internal Audit | Conduct systematic internal audits of the AIMS | ISO 27001 Clause 9.2 — Internal Audit | **Direct reuse** — The internal audit program, audit methodology, audit report format, and auditor competency framework are fully reusable. | Extend the existing audit program to include AIMS audit cycles. Add ISO 42001 to the audit scope. Train existing internal auditors on ISO 42001 requirements (or engage external support for the first AIMS audit cycle). This is one of the highest-leverage reuse opportunities — no new audit infrastructure needed. |
| **Clause 9.3** — Management Review | Conduct periodic management review of the AIMS | ISO 27001 Clause 9.3 — Management Review | **Direct reuse** — The management review process, meeting cadence, agenda structure, and record format are fully reusable. | Add AIMS agenda items to existing management review meetings: AI risk status, AI policy compliance, AI objectives performance, AI incident summary, AI regulatory developments. Maintain a combined management review record that covers both ISMS and AIMS inputs and outputs. This avoids meeting duplication and demonstrates integrated governance. |
| **Clause 10.1** — Nonconformity and Corrective Action | Identify nonconformities, take corrective action, prevent recurrence | ISO 27001 Clause 10.1 — Nonconformity and Corrective Action | **Direct reuse** — The NCAR process, root cause analysis methodology, corrective action tracking system, and closure verification process are fully reusable. | Extend the existing NCAR process to cover AIMS nonconformities. Add ISO 42001 as a reference standard in the NCAR form. No new corrective action infrastructure needed. This is one of the highest-leverage reuse opportunities. |

## Using This Matrix

### How to Use Table 1 (ISO 42001 ↔ EU AI Act)

**Primary use case:** Client asks "We're subject to the EU AI Act — does our ISO 42001 certification help us?"

1. **Establish applicability first.** Before using this table, determine whether the client's AI systems are classified as high-risk under Annex III of the EU AI Act. The mapping is most relevant for high-risk AI deployers. For limited-risk or minimal-risk AI, the EU AI Act obligations are lighter and the mapping is less critical.

2. **Use the "Mapping Strength" column to prioritize.** Direct mappings mean the ISO 42001 work product can serve double duty with minimal adaptation. Partial mappings require additional work to meet the EU AI Act's specific requirements.

3. **Flag the gaps.** The most important gaps to highlight for clients are:
   - **Art. 27 FRIA** — The ISO 42001 impact assessment is a foundation, but the FRIA has specific legal content and registration requirements that go beyond ISO 42001.
   - **Art. 73 incident reporting** — ISO 42001 corrective action covers internal nonconformities; Art. 73 adds an external reporting obligation to national authorities.
   - **Art. 50 transparency** — Specific disclosure requirements for chatbots, emotion recognition, and deepfakes that go beyond A.8.2's general disclosure requirement.

4. **Communicate the value clearly.** ISO 42001 certification demonstrates systematic AI governance, which is strong evidence of good-faith compliance effort under the EU AI Act. It does not, however, constitute legal compliance or substitute for legal counsel.

### How to Use Table 2 (ISO 42001 ↔ NIST AI RMF)

**Primary use case:** Client's US parent company, board, or investors ask about NIST AI RMF alignment.

1. **Explain the framework relationship.** NIST AI RMF is voluntary and outcome-oriented; ISO 42001 is a certifiable management system standard. They are complementary, not competing. ISO 42001 provides the management system structure; NIST AI RMF provides a risk-focused analytical lens.

2. **Use the NIST function mapping to demonstrate coverage.** Show clients that their ISO 42001 implementation covers all four NIST AI RMF functions (GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE). This is a strong message for US-facing stakeholders.

3. **Identify enhancement opportunities.** Where mappings are "Partial," the notes column identifies specific NIST AI RMF requirements that go beyond ISO 42001. These are typically enhancements to existing processes (e.g., adding empirical evidence to risk assessments for MAP 2.1/2.2) rather than new processes.

4. **Do not claim NIST AI RMF conformance.** ISO 42001 certification does not constitute NIST AI RMF conformance. If a client needs to formally demonstrate NIST AI RMF alignment (e.g., for a US federal contract), a separate NIST AI RMF assessment is required.

### How to Use Table 3 (ISO 42001 ↔ ISO 27001 Leverage)

**Primary use case:** Client is ISO 27001 certified and asks "How much extra work is ISO 42001?"

1. **Lead with the good news.** ISO 27001-certified clients have a significant head start. The management system infrastructure — document control, risk methodology, audit program, management review, corrective action — is already in place and audited. Estimated effort reduction: **30–40%** compared to a greenfield implementation.

2. **Use the "Action Required" column to scope the work.** The action required column describes exactly what needs to be done for each leverage opportunity. Use this to build the project plan and effort estimate.

3. **Distinguish reuse from extension.** "Direct reuse" means the existing process/document can be used with minor additions. "Partial reuse" means the existing process provides a foundation but new AI-specific elements must be added. Neither means the ISO 27001 control satisfies the ISO 42001 requirement without any action.

4. **Highest-leverage opportunities for ISO 27001 clients:**
   - **Document control (Clause 7.5)** — Extend existing system; no new infrastructure needed.
   - **Internal audit (Clause 9.2)** — Extend existing audit program; train existing auditors.
   - **Management review (Clause 9.3)** — Add AIMS agenda items to existing meetings.
   - **Corrective action (Clause 10.1)** — Extend existing NCAR process.
   - **Supplier management (A.10.2 ↔ A.5.19–5.22)** — Extend existing vendor assessment.

5. **Important caveat for clients.** Emphasize that ISO 27001 certification does not satisfy ISO 42001 requirements. The leverage is real and significant, but ISO 42001 certification requires a separate audit against ISO 42001 requirements. The two certifications are complementary, not substitutable.

6. **Scope the AI-specific work clearly.** The effort that cannot be reused from ISO 27001 is the AI-specific content: AI policy drafting, AI risk assessment (AI-specific risk categories), AI impact assessment, AI system documentation, human oversight procedures, and AI awareness training content. This is where the implementation effort concentrates.

## Mapping Strength Definitions

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Direct** | The ISO 42001 requirement and the mapped framework requirement address the same governance need with substantially the same approach. ISO 42001 work products can serve as primary evidence with minimal adaptation. |
| **Partial** | The ISO 42001 requirement and the mapped framework requirement overlap significantly but differ in scope, specificity, or emphasis. ISO 42001 work products provide a strong foundation but require supplementation to fully address the mapped requirement. |
| **Indirect** | The ISO 42001 requirement and the mapped framework requirement address related but distinct governance needs. ISO 42001 work demonstrates relevant governance maturity but does not directly address the mapped requirement. |
