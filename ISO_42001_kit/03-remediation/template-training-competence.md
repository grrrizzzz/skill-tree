> **HOW TO CUSTOMIZE (Remove this block before client delivery)**
>
> This template covers three interconnected requirements: the competency framework (what people need to know), the training record (evidence they learned it), and the awareness program (how you keep knowledge current). Complete them in that order.
>
> **Step 1 — Competency Framework (Part 1):** Review the role-based table in Section 1.3. Add or remove rows to match the client's actual org structure. The eight roles listed are typical for SMBs; a 15-person company may collapse several into one person. Adjust competency levels accordingly.
>
> **Step 2 — Training Record (Part 2):** Replace the example rows with real employee data. Every person who uses an AI tool within AIMS scope needs at least one training record entry. "All Staff" training is the most common gap — if it hasn't happened yet, record the planned date and update after delivery.
>
> **Step 3 — Training Program (Part 3):** The four modules are designed to be delivered in-house. Module 1 can be a 45-minute all-hands session or a recorded video. Module 4 is the exception — recommend external ISO 42001 lead auditor training for the AIMS Owner.
>
> **Step 4 — Awareness Program (Part 4):** This is ongoing, not a one-time event. Help the client assign an owner for each activity before you leave.
>
> **Step 5 — Competency Summary (Part 5):** Fill in the numbers after initial training is complete. This table becomes audit evidence that the organization has assessed its competency gaps and has a plan to close them.
>
> **Common mistakes to avoid:**
> - Don't list training as "completed" without evidence (certificate, attendance record, or signed acknowledgment)
> - Don't skip the assessment result column — even a simple pass/fail quiz counts
> - Don't forget contractors and temporary staff who use AI tools within scope

## Part 1: AI Competency Framework

### 1.1 Purpose

This competency framework defines the AI-related knowledge and skills required across the organization. Its purpose is threefold: to establish clear expectations for each role, to identify gaps between current and required competency, and to guide training planning so resources are directed where they matter most.

The framework focuses on responsible AI use, not AI development. The organization uses third-party AI tools and systems. Competency requirements reflect that role: understanding how to use AI tools appropriately, recognizing their limitations, handling data correctly, and knowing when and how to escalate concerns.

This document is reviewed annually and updated whenever the organization adopts new AI tools, changes its AI governance structure, or identifies competency gaps through internal audit or incident review.

---

### 1.2 Competency Levels

Four levels apply across all roles. Each level builds on the one below it.

| Level | Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Awareness | Understands what AI is, why governance matters, and what the organization's AI policy requires. Knows what data can and cannot be entered into AI tools. Knows how to report concerns. No technical depth required. |
| **2** | Practitioner | Uses AI tools responsibly within their role. Understands the specific tools they work with, their limitations, and the data handling rules that apply. Can recognize and report problematic outputs. Applies the Acceptable Use Policy in day-to-day work. |
| **3** | Advanced | Manages AI systems or AI governance activities. Understands the AIMS structure, can conduct or support risk assessments, and can evaluate AI vendors. Responsible for ensuring their team or function meets competency requirements. |
| **4** | Expert | Leads AIMS implementation and maintenance. Deep knowledge of ISO 42001 requirements, internal audit methodology, and AI risk management. Capable of facilitating management review and driving continual improvement. |

## Part 3: Training Program Outline

This section defines the training modules the organization delivers to meet Clauses 7.2 and 7.3 and Annex A controls A.4.3 and A.4.4. Modules are designed for in-house delivery. External training is recommended only for Module 4.

---

### Module 1: AI Governance Awareness

**Audience:** All staff, including contractors and temporary workers who use AI tools within scope

**Duration:** 30 to 60 minutes

**Delivery:** All-hands session, recorded video, or e-learning. Must be completed before an employee uses any AI tool within scope.

**Frequency:** On joining; annually thereafter; when the AI policy or Acceptable Use Policy is materially updated.

**Content:**

1. What is AI? A plain-language explanation of what AI tools do and don't do, using examples from tools the organization actually uses (e.g., Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT, Salesforce Einstein).

2. Why governance matters. Brief explanation of why the organization has an AI policy: to protect employees, customers, and the business from the risks that come with AI use.

3. The AI policy and Acceptable Use Policy. Key rules employees must follow: what tools are approved, what data can and cannot be entered, what outputs require human review before use.

4. Data handling rules. Specific guidance on what constitutes sensitive data (personal data, financial data, confidential business information) and why it must not be entered into public AI tools without authorization.

5. How to report concerns. The process for reporting AI-related concerns, unexpected outputs, or suspected policy violations. Who to contact, what information to provide, and what happens next.

6. Consequences of policy violations. Clear statement that misuse of AI tools is a disciplinary matter, consistent with other IT and data handling policies.

**Assessment:** Short quiz (5 to 10 questions) or signed acknowledgment confirming the employee has read and understood the AI policy and Acceptable Use Policy.

---

### Module 2: Responsible AI Use

**Audience:** All employees who regularly use AI tools as part of their role (AI tool users)

**Duration:** 60 to 90 minutes

**Delivery:** Facilitated workshop or structured self-study with practical exercises. Ideally delivered in small groups by function so examples are relevant to participants' actual work.

**Frequency:** On joining or when first assigned to use an AI tool; annually thereafter; when a new AI tool is adopted.

**Content:**

1. Understanding AI limitations. What AI tools can and can't do reliably. The concept of hallucination: why AI tools sometimes produce confident-sounding but incorrect outputs. Why human oversight is not optional.

2. Human oversight requirements. Which outputs require human review before use or distribution. How to verify AI-generated content, calculations, or recommendations. The organization's specific oversight requirements for each tool in use.

3. Data handling for specific tools. Practical guidance on the AI tools this audience uses: what data is permitted, what is prohibited, and why. Worked examples using realistic scenarios from the function.

4. Recognizing problematic outputs. How to identify outputs that may be biased, inaccurate, or inappropriate. What "unexpected output" means in practice. The difference between a minor error and something that needs to be escalated.

5. Reporting process. Reinforcement of the reporting process from Module 1, with function-specific examples of what to report and to whom.

**Assessment:** Practical exercise: participants review a set of AI-generated outputs and identify which require human review, which contain errors, and which would violate the Acceptable Use Policy. Facilitator reviews responses and provides feedback.

---

### Module 3: AI Risk and Governance

**Audience:** AIMS Owner, IT Lead, Department Heads

**Duration:** 3 to 4 hours (half-day workshop)

**Delivery:** Facilitated workshop. Can be split across two sessions if scheduling requires it.

**Frequency:** Once during AIMS implementation; refresher when ISO 42001 is revised or when the organization's AI use changes significantly.

**Content:**

1. ISO 42001 requirements overview. The structure of the standard: context, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation, improvement. What each clause requires and how it applies to an AI user organization.

2. AI risk assessment methodology. How to identify AI-related risks, assess likelihood and impact, and select appropriate controls. Walkthrough of the organization's risk assessment register. Practical exercise using a real or hypothetical AI tool.

3. Supplier and vendor assessment. Why third-party AI providers are a supply chain risk. How to assess AI vendors: what questions to ask, what documentation to request, what red flags to look for. Overview of the organization's supplier assessment process.

4. Internal audit basics. What an internal audit is and isn't. How to plan a simple AIMS internal audit. What evidence to collect and how to record findings. The difference between a minor and major nonconformity.

**Assessment:** Participants complete a short risk assessment for one of the organization's AI tools using the organization's risk assessment template. Facilitator reviews and provides feedback.

---

### Module 4: AIMS Management

**Audience:** AIMS Owner

**Duration:** 8 hours or more (full-day or multi-day external course)

**Delivery:** External training from an accredited provider is strongly recommended. In-house delivery is not appropriate for this module given the depth of knowledge required.

**Recommended providers:** BSI Group, Bureau Veritas, DNV, LRQA, or other accredited ISO 42001 training providers. Look for courses that include ISO 42001 internal auditor or lead auditor certification.

**Frequency:** Once before AIMS certification; refresher when the standard is revised.

**Content:**

1. Full ISO 42001 standard requirements. Clause-by-clause analysis of the standard, including all normative requirements and Annex A controls. Understanding which controls apply to AI user organizations and how to justify exclusions in the Statement of Applicability.

2. AIMS implementation methodology. How to build an AIMS from scratch: scoping, gap analysis, risk assessment, control selection, documentation, and readiness review. Common implementation pitfalls and how to avoid them.

3. Internal audit lead auditor skills. How to plan, conduct, and report an internal audit. Interviewing techniques. Evidence collection and evaluation. Writing clear, actionable findings. Managing auditee relationships.

4. Management review facilitation. How to structure and run a management review meeting. What inputs are required. How to document outputs and track resulting actions. Connecting management review to continual improvement.

**Assessment:** External certification assessment as part of the training course (e.g., ISO 42001 Internal Auditor certificate).

## Part 4: Awareness Program

Training is a one-time or periodic event. Awareness is ongoing. This section defines the activities that keep AI governance visible and current between formal training cycles.

The AIMS Owner is responsible for coordinating the awareness program. Individual activities may be delegated to HR, IT, or department heads as appropriate.

---

### Quarterly AI Governance Update

**Frequency:** Every quarter

**Format:** Brief written update (email or intranet post), 300 to 500 words

**Content:** Summary of any changes to approved AI tools, updates to the AI policy or Acceptable Use Policy, any AI-related incidents or near-misses (anonymized), and any relevant regulatory developments. The goal is to keep AI governance visible without creating communication fatigue.

**Owner:** AIMS Owner

---

### Annual Policy Acknowledgment

**Frequency:** Annually, aligned with the policy review cycle

**Format:** All staff sign (physically or electronically) an updated Acceptable Use Policy acknowledgment confirming they have read the current version and understand their obligations.

**Process:** HR distributes the acknowledgment. Signed copies are retained as documented information. Non-responses are followed up within 10 business days. The AIMS Owner receives a completion report.

**Owner:** HR, coordinated by AIMS Owner

---

### Incident Debrief Communications

**Frequency:** Following any AI-related incident or significant near-miss

**Format:** Brief communication to affected staff (and all staff if the incident has broad relevance) explaining what happened, what the impact was, what was done to address it, and what staff should do differently going forward.

**Purpose:** Incidents are learning opportunities. Sharing lessons learned (without blame) reinforces the importance of reporting and builds a culture where staff feel safe raising concerns.

**Owner:** AIMS Owner, in coordination with the relevant department head

---

### New Tool Onboarding

**Frequency:** Whenever a new AI tool is approved for use

**Format:** Briefing session or written guidance note covering the tool's purpose, approved use cases, data handling rules specific to that tool, and any limitations staff should know about.

**Requirement:** No staff member uses a new AI tool within scope until they have completed new tool onboarding. This is a control requirement, not a recommendation.

**Owner:** IT Lead (technical briefing), AIMS Owner (policy and governance elements)

---

### Regulatory Update Briefings

**Frequency:** When significant regulatory changes occur (e.g., EU AI Act implementation milestones, new sector-specific AI guidance)

**Format:** Summary briefing for AIMS Owner and Senior Management; simplified summary for all staff if the change affects day-to-day AI use.

**Purpose:** The regulatory landscape for AI is changing quickly. Staff who use AI tools in customer-facing or high-stakes contexts need to know when the rules change.

**Owner:** AIMS Owner, with input from legal counsel as appropriate
