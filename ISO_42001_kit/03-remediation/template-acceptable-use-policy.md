## 1. Purpose
*ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | Controls A.6.2.10, A.9.3, A.9.4, A.4.4*

This Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) governs the responsible use of AI tools and systems by all personnel of [Organization Name]. It translates the Organization's AI governance commitments — set out in the AI Policy (AIMS-POL-001) — into practical, day-to-day rules for staff.

AI tools offer genuine productivity benefits. This policy is not designed to prevent their use. It is designed to ensure that when staff use AI tools, they do so in ways that:

- Protect the privacy and data of customers, employees, and the Organization
- Comply with applicable laws and regulations
- Maintain the Organization's accountability for decisions and outputs
- Prevent harm to individuals and the Organization's reputation
- Support the Organization's ISO/IEC 42001:2023 certification obligations

Every employee, contractor, and third party who uses AI tools on behalf of [Organization Name] is responsible for understanding and complying with this policy.

---

## 2. Scope
*ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | Controls A.6.2.10, A.9.3*

### 2.1 Who This Policy Applies To

This policy applies to:

- All employees of [Organization Name], including full-time, part-time, and casual employees
- Contractors, consultants, and temporary workers engaged by the Organization
- Third-party service providers who use AI tools to deliver services to or on behalf of the Organization
- Any other individual who accesses the Organization's systems, data, or AI tools

### 2.2 What This Policy Covers

This policy applies to:

- All AI tools on the Organization's Approved AI Tools list (Section 7)
- Any AI tool used with organizational data, systems, or on organizational premises, regardless of whether it is on the approved list
- AI features embedded in software platforms the Organization uses (e.g., AI-assisted drafting in email clients, AI-powered search in document management systems)

### 2.3 What This Policy Does Not Cover

This policy does not govern:

- Personal use of AI tools on personal devices with no organizational data involved
- AI tools used exclusively by third-party vendors in their own operations, where the Organization has no control over or input to those systems

*If you are unsure whether a particular AI tool or use case falls within this policy, contact IT or your manager before proceeding.*

## 3. Definitions
*ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — Clause 3 | Controlled Vocabulary: AIMS-GLOSS-001*

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **AI System** | An engineered system that generates outputs such as predictions, recommendations, decisions, or content that influence real or virtual environments, operating with varying levels of autonomy. |
| **AI Tool** | A specific software application or feature incorporating AI system capabilities, used to perform business tasks. Examples: Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT Enterprise, Salesforce Einstein, Grammarly Business. |
| **Shadow AI** | The use of AI tools by employees without organizational knowledge, approval, or governance controls. This includes using personal AI accounts for work purposes, using free-tier AI tools with company data, or using AI tools not on the approved list. |
| **Sensitive Data** | Any data that, if disclosed, could harm an individual or the Organization. Includes: customer personally identifiable information (PII), employee personal data, financial records, legal documents, trade secrets, and any data classified as Confidential or Restricted under the Organization's data classification policy. |
| **High-Stakes Decision** | A decision that materially affects an individual's rights, opportunities, or wellbeing. Examples: hiring or termination decisions, credit or service eligibility decisions, performance assessments, disciplinary actions. |
| **Approved AI Tool** | An AI tool that has been assessed, approved, and listed in the Organization's Approved AI Tools Register. Only approved tools may be used with organizational data. |
| **Prompt** | The input text or instruction provided to a generative AI tool. Prompts may contain sensitive data if not carefully constructed. |

## 4. Permitted Uses
*ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | Controls A.9.3, A.9.4*

### 4.1 General Permitted Uses

Staff may use approved AI tools for the following general purposes:

- Drafting, editing, and summarizing documents, emails, and reports (subject to human review before sending or publishing)
- Research assistance and information gathering (subject to verification of AI-generated facts)
- Generating ideas, outlines, and creative content for internal use (subject to review)
- Automating repetitive tasks such as data formatting, scheduling, and routine correspondence
- Analyzing data and generating insights to support (not replace) human decision-making
- Learning and professional development activities

### 4.2 Permitted Uses by Department

The following examples illustrate permitted AI tool uses by department. This list is illustrative, not exhaustive. When in doubt, consult your manager or IT.

| Department | Permitted AI Tool Uses |
|---|---|
| **Sales** | Drafting prospect outreach emails (reviewed before sending); summarizing call notes; generating proposal templates; researching prospect companies using publicly available information |
| **HR** | Drafting job descriptions (reviewed before posting); summarizing interview notes for internal use; generating policy document drafts (reviewed by HR lead before distribution); creating training materials |
| **Finance** | Summarizing financial reports for internal briefings; drafting routine financial communications; generating data analysis templates; formatting spreadsheet data |
| **Marketing** | Drafting marketing copy (reviewed and approved before publication); generating content ideas and outlines; summarizing market research; creating social media post drafts (reviewed before posting) |
| **Operations** | Drafting standard operating procedures (reviewed before distribution); summarizing meeting notes; generating process improvement ideas; creating project status report templates |
| **Customer Service** | Drafting response templates for common customer queries (reviewed before use); summarizing customer feedback; generating FAQ content (reviewed before publication) |

### 4.3 Conditions for All Permitted Uses

All permitted uses are subject to the following conditions:

1. **Human review:** AI-generated content must be reviewed by a qualified human before it is sent to customers, published, or used to inform a significant decision.
2. **Fact verification:** AI-generated factual claims must be independently verified before reliance. AI tools can and do produce inaccurate information.
3. **Data handling:** Only data permitted for the tool's approved data classification may be entered (see Section 6 and Section 7).
4. **Approved tools only:** Only tools on the Approved AI Tools list may be used with organizational data.

## 5. Prohibited Uses
*ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | Controls A.6.2.10, A.9.3*

The following uses of AI tools are **strictly prohibited**. Violations may result in disciplinary action (see Section 12).

### 5.1 Data and Privacy Prohibitions

**5.1.1** Entering customer personally identifiable information (PII) — including names, contact details, account numbers, financial data, or health information — into any AI tool not explicitly approved for that data classification.

*Example of prohibited use: Copying a customer's full name, email address, and purchase history into a free-tier ChatGPT account to generate a personalized email.*

**5.1.2** Using personal AI accounts (e.g., a personal ChatGPT free account, personal Google Gemini account) for any work purpose that involves organizational data, customer data, or confidential business information.

*Example of prohibited use: Using your personal ChatGPT account to summarize a confidential client proposal because your work account is unavailable.*

**5.1.3** Entering employee personal data — including performance records, salary information, health data, or disciplinary records — into AI tools without explicit authorization from HR and confirmation that the tool is approved for that data classification.

**5.1.4** Sharing confidential business information — including trade secrets, unreleased product plans, financial forecasts, or merger and acquisition information — with AI tools without written authorization from the AIMS Owner or designated approver.

### 5.2 Decision-Making Prohibitions

**5.2.1** Using AI tools to make final decisions about individuals — including hiring, termination, promotion, performance assessment, credit decisions, or service denial — without mandatory human review and sign-off by an authorized decision-maker.

*Example of prohibited use: Using an AI tool to screen job applications and rejecting candidates based solely on AI-generated scores without human review of each rejection.*

**5.2.2** Using AI tools to make or recommend financial commitments, contract approvals, or procurement decisions above [insert threshold, e.g., $5,000] without human review and approval through the standard authorization process.

### 5.3 Content and Representation Prohibitions

**5.3.1** Using AI tools to generate content that misrepresents the Organization, its products, services, or positions — including fabricated testimonials, false claims, or misleading statistics.

**5.3.2** Using AI tools to generate content that impersonates another person, organization, or authority.

**5.3.3** Publishing or distributing AI-generated content as if it were human-authored, where the context requires disclosure of AI involvement (e.g., regulated communications, legal documents, formal reports to regulators).

**5.3.4** Using AI tools to generate content that is discriminatory, harassing, defamatory, or otherwise unlawful.

### 5.4 Tool and Access Prohibitions

**5.4.1** Using AI tools not on the Approved AI Tools list with any organizational data, without prior written approval from IT and the AIMS Owner.

**5.4.2** Circumventing, disabling, or attempting to bypass any technical controls, access restrictions, or usage monitoring applied to approved AI tools.

**5.4.3** Sharing login credentials for approved AI tools with colleagues, contractors, or third parties. Each user must have their own authorized account.

**5.4.4** Using AI tools to process data in jurisdictions or on infrastructure not permitted under the Organization's data residency requirements or applicable law.

## 6. Data Handling Requirements
*ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | Controls A.6.2.10, A.9.4*

### 6.1 Data Classification and AI Tool Use

The type of data that may be entered into an AI tool depends on the tool's approved data classification. Refer to the Approved AI Tools table (Section 7) for each tool's permitted data classification.

| Data Classification | Description | Examples | AI Tool Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Public** | Information approved for public release | Published marketing materials, public website content, publicly available research | May be entered into any approved AI tool |
| **Internal** | Information for internal use only; not sensitive if disclosed | Internal meeting notes, general process documents, non-sensitive project plans | May be entered into AI tools approved for Internal data |
| **Confidential** | Sensitive business information; disclosure could harm the Organization | Financial forecasts, client contracts, strategic plans, employee salary data | May only be entered into AI tools explicitly approved for Confidential data |
| **Restricted / PII** | Highly sensitive personal or regulated data; strict access controls apply | Customer PII, employee personal data, health information, payment card data | May only be entered into AI tools explicitly approved for Restricted data, with additional controls |

### 6.2 Prompt Hygiene

When using generative AI tools, staff must apply the following prompt hygiene practices:

- **Anonymize where possible:** Replace real names, account numbers, and identifying details with placeholders (e.g., "Customer A" instead of a real name) when the specific identity is not required for the task.
- **Minimum necessary data:** Include only the data necessary to accomplish the task. Do not paste entire documents or datasets when a summary or excerpt would suffice.
- **Review before submitting:** Before submitting a prompt, review it for sensitive data that should not be included.

### 6.3 AI Tool Data Retention

Staff should be aware that many AI tools retain prompts and outputs for varying periods. Before entering any data into an AI tool, confirm the tool's data retention and usage practices in the Approved AI Tools table (Section 7) or by contacting IT.

## 8. Shadow AI
*ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | Controls A.6.2.10, A.9.3, A.3.3*

### 8.1 What Is Shadow AI?

Shadow AI refers to the use of AI tools by employees without organizational knowledge, approval, or governance controls. Common examples include:

- Using a personal ChatGPT, Google Gemini, or Claude account for work tasks involving company data
- Using a free-tier AI tool that has not been assessed or approved by IT
- Using an AI feature within a software tool that IT and compliance have not reviewed
- Building or using AI-powered automations (e.g., in Zapier, Make, or Power Automate) without IT approval

### 8.2 Why Shadow AI Is Prohibited

Shadow AI creates risks that the Organization cannot manage:

- **Data leakage:** Free-tier AI tools typically use submitted data to train their models. Customer PII or confidential business information entered into an unapproved tool may be retained, used for training, or exposed in a data breach.
- **Compliance failure:** Using unapproved tools with regulated data (e.g., personal data subject to GDPR) may constitute a data protection breach, triggering notification obligations and regulatory penalties.
- **Accountability gaps:** If an AI-generated output causes harm and the tool was not approved or monitored, the Organization has no audit trail and no contractual recourse against the vendor.
- **Reputational risk:** AI-generated content produced through unapproved tools may not meet the Organization's quality, accuracy, or ethical standards.

### 8.3 How to Report Shadow AI

If you discover or suspect that shadow AI is being used within the Organization:

1. **Do not confront the individual directly.**
2. Report the concern to your manager or to IT using the AI Incident Reporting process (AIMS-INC-001).
3. Reports can also be made anonymously through [insert reporting channel, e.g., the Organization's ethics hotline or anonymous reporting form].

Reports made in good faith will not result in retaliation against the reporter. The Organization is committed to creating an environment where AI concerns can be raised without fear.

### 8.4 Requesting Approval for a New Tool

If you believe a new AI tool would benefit your work, the correct process is to request approval — not to start using it. Submit an AI Tool Request (AIMS-TOOL-REQ-001) to IT. IT will assess the tool and, if appropriate, add it to the approved list. This process typically takes [insert timeframe, e.g., 5–10 business days].

## 9. Human Oversight Requirements
*ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | Control A.9.5*

### 9.1 The Principle

AI tools are decision-support tools. They assist human judgment; they do not replace it. The Organization maintains a firm commitment to human oversight of AI-assisted processes, particularly where AI outputs influence decisions that affect individuals or carry significant risk.

### 9.2 Mandatory Human Review Scenarios

Human review by a qualified, authorized person is **mandatory** before acting on AI outputs in the following scenarios:

| Scenario | Required Reviewer | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hiring decisions** — using AI to screen, score, or rank candidates | HR Manager or designated hiring manager | Every candidate rejection informed by AI output must be reviewed by a human. AI scores are inputs, not decisions. |
| **Performance assessments** — using AI to analyze or summarize employee performance data | Direct manager + HR | AI-generated performance summaries must be reviewed and validated before inclusion in formal assessments. |
| **Customer-facing communications** — AI-generated emails, letters, or messages to customers | Sender (employee) + manager for high-value or sensitive communications | All AI-generated customer communications must be read and approved by the sending employee before dispatch. |
| **Financial decisions** — AI-assisted analysis informing expenditure, credit, or pricing decisions above [e.g., $5,000] | Finance Manager or authorized approver | AI analysis is an input to the decision; the decision must be made and documented by an authorized human. |
| **Legal or regulatory submissions** — AI-assisted drafting of documents submitted to regulators, courts, or counterparties | Legal / Compliance + document owner | AI-generated content in legal documents must be reviewed for accuracy and appropriateness by a qualified reviewer. |
| **Service denial decisions** — using AI to determine customer eligibility or deny service | Customer Service Manager or designated approver | Customers must not be denied service based solely on AI output without human review. |

### 9.3 Documenting Human Review

Where mandatory human review is required, the reviewer must document their review. Documentation requirements are set out in the relevant departmental procedure. At minimum, the record should note: who reviewed, when, and that the AI output was reviewed before action was taken.

### 9.4 When AI Outputs Seem Wrong

If an AI tool produces an output that seems incorrect, biased, harmful, or unexpected:

- **Do not act on the output** without additional verification.
- **Do not dismiss the concern** — unexpected AI outputs may indicate a problem with the tool, the data, or the use case.
- **Report the output** using the AI Incident Reporting process (AIMS-INC-001).

## 10. Reporting Obligations
*ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | Controls A.3.3, A.9.4*

### 10.1 What to Report

All personnel must report the following through the AI Incident Reporting process (AIMS-INC-001):

- AI tool outputs that appear to be inaccurate, biased, discriminatory, or harmful
- Suspected or confirmed use of unapproved AI tools with organizational data (shadow AI)
- Suspected or confirmed data leakage through an AI tool
- AI-generated content that was sent to customers or published before adequate human review
- Any situation where an AI tool was used to make a decision about an individual without the required human review
- Vendor notifications of AI system incidents, breaches, or material changes
- Any other AI-related concern that may affect the Organization's compliance, reputation, or the wellbeing of individuals

### 10.2 How to Report

### 10.3 Response Timeframes

| Severity | Description | Target Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | Active data breach, PII exposed, regulatory notification likely required | Immediate — contact IT and AIMS Owner directly |
| **High** | Significant policy violation, harmful AI output acted upon, shadow AI with sensitive data | Within 24 hours |

## 12. Consequences of Non-Compliance
*ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | Controls A.6.2.10, A.9.4*

### 12.1 Disciplinary Process

Violations of this policy are treated as misconduct and are subject to the Organization's standard disciplinary process, as set out in [insert reference to HR disciplinary policy]. The severity of the disciplinary response will be proportionate to the nature and impact of the violation.

### 12.2 Severity Guidance

| Violation Type | Likely Disciplinary Response |
|---|---|
| Minor first-time violation (e.g., using an unapproved tool with non-sensitive internal data, failing to complete training on time) | Verbal or written warning; mandatory remedial training |
| Moderate violation (e.g., using an unapproved tool with confidential data, failing to apply human review to a non-critical AI output) | Written warning; mandatory remedial training; temporary suspension of AI tool access |
| Serious violation (e.g., entering customer PII into an unapproved tool, using AI to make a high-stakes decision without human review, deliberate circumvention of controls) | Final written warning or termination; potential regulatory notification; legal action where applicable |
| Repeated violations | Escalating disciplinary action up to and including termination |

### 12.3 Third-Party Violations

Contractors, consultants, and third-party service providers who violate this policy may have their access to the Organization's systems and data revoked. Violations may also constitute a breach of contract, with consequences as set out in the relevant service agreement.

### 12.4 Good Faith Reporting

Employees who report policy violations or AI concerns in good faith are protected from retaliation, regardless of the outcome of any investigation.
