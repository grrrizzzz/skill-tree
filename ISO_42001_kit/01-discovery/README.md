# Phase 1: Discovery & Scoping

> **Directory:** `01-discovery/` | **Internal Reference**

---

## What This Phase Accomplishes

Discovery is where the engagement gets grounded. Before any gap analysis or remediation work can happen, you need a clear picture of the client's organization, their AI systems, and the boundaries of what the AIMS will cover. This phase produces three things: a complete inventory of AI systems in scope, a register of interested parties, and a signed AIMS scope statement. Without these, every subsequent phase is built on assumptions. The discovery procedure walks you through stakeholder interviews, AI system identification workshops, and the scope definition process. Plan for two to four working sessions with the client.

## Phase Inputs

Before starting Phase 1, you need:

- Signed engagement proposal or Statement of Work
- Client point of contact with authority to convene stakeholders
- Basic organizational information (org chart, business units, primary AI tools in use)
- Access to `00-foundation/glossary.md` and `00-foundation/annex-a-reference.md` for reference during scoping conversations

---

## Phase Outputs

Phase 1 produces:

- **AI System Inventory** (`template-ai-system-inventory.md`, completed) — a documented list of every AI system in scope, with enough detail to support risk assessment in Phase 3
- **Stakeholder Register** (`template-stakeholder-register.md`, completed) — a record of interested parties and their relevance to the AIMS
- **AIMS Scope Statement** — a written, agreed definition of what the AIMS covers. Typically captured within the AI System Inventory or as a standalone section. The client's leadership should review and sign off before Phase 2 begins.

---

## Estimated Duration

**1–2 weeks** for a typical SMB engagement. This assumes two to three discovery workshops (60–90 minutes each), plus time to document outputs and get client sign-off on scope.

Engagements with many business units, complex AI deployments, or unclear organizational boundaries may take longer. Don't rush scope definition — a poorly defined scope creates problems at every subsequent phase and at the certification audit.

## Notes for This Phase

Most SMB clients significantly undercount their AI systems at the start of an engagement. Embedded AI features within SaaS platforms (CRM tools, HR systems, email clients) are often overlooked. The discovery procedure includes techniques for surfacing these hidden systems. Budget time for a second pass after the initial workshop — clients frequently remember additional tools once they've had a few days to think about it.

Scope definition is the most consequential decision in the engagement. A scope that's too broad creates unnecessary work and makes certification harder. A scope that's too narrow may not satisfy the client's regulatory or commercial objectives. The procedure includes a scope boundary decision framework to help navigate this.
